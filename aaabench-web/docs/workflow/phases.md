# Production phases and their exit gates

The order matters more than the schedule. Each gate is a thing you can photograph or measure, not
a feeling.

## 0. Decide the place (hours, not days)

The region, the water, the terrain, the reason anyone stopped here, the era, the economy, the
name. Written down, in the repo, before anything is built.

**Gate:** a one-page brief that says what caused this city, and a hand-drawn-in-text map of where
the districts go and why. Someone else could build from it.

## 1. Geography and the road network

Terrain and water first — they decide everything else. Then the primary road network, then
secondaries, then the district boundaries that fall out of them.

**Gate:** the `aerial` pose reads as a *plan*: you can see the hierarchy of roads, where the
centre is, where the water is, and where each district sits. Nothing is dressed. Nothing needs to
be.

## 2. Blockout

Block footprints, massing, heights, street sections. Grey boxes, correct dimensions, correct
proportions. This is where scale errors are cheap to fix; after this they are expensive.

**Gate:** `street` pose at eye height shows a street whose *proportions* are right — kerb, road,
pavement width, storey height, setback. Compare against a real photograph. Also: you can walk it,
and the walk takes a plausible amount of time.

## 3. Systems, thin

Player and camera, traffic, pedestrians, day/night, the game's screens, save. All of them at their
crudest, all of them working, all of them within budget.

**Gate:** a `play` session that starts at a title screen, walks, drives or rides something, opens
a map, and comes back — with no console errors and the camera actually moving.

## 4. Set dressing, in a gradient

The pass that turns blockout into a place: façade detail, ground clutter, signage, street
furniture, vegetation, wear. Highest quality where the player will spend time, decreasing outward.

**Gate:** three random blocks in the core, photographed at eye height, survive a comparison with
a real photograph of a comparable street. The far edge still reads correctly from the air.

## 5. Lighting and atmosphere

Sun position through the day, sky, fog with distance, artificial lights that are sources rather
than glows, night that has structure.

**Gate:** the same pose at dawn / noon / dusk / night reads as four different times, not four
grades of one image.

## 6. Content

Missions, cast, dialogue, the writing. This can start much earlier — the writing is where the
design happens — but it is not finished until there is something to do in the place.

**Gate:** a stranger could be handed the game and would have somewhere to go, a reason to go
there, and someone to talk to when they arrive.

## 7. Polish, forever

Performance, the audit loop, the tells, the things you have stopped seeing. There is no gate,
because there is no end. Re-shoot the sweep, read it cold, fix the worst thing, commit.

---

## What phases do not mean

- **They are not sequential per-district.** Breadth first: the whole map gets phase 1 before any
  district gets phase 4. A finished district next to an empty plane is a worse artifact than a
  rough city, and it is also a worse *test*, because it hides whether you can hold a whole place
  in your head.
- **They are not a plan you follow off a cliff.** If phase 2 reveals that the geography is wrong,
  go back to phase 1. The gate exists to catch exactly that.
- **They do not end.** Each pass over the map lifts the whole gradient. Two shallow passes beat
  one deep one that never reaches the edges.
